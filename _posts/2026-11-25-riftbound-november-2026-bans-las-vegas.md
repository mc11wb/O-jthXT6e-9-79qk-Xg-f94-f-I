Riftbound's competitive scene is heading into a major shake-up. On November 23, 2026, eight cards and two battlefields joined the Standard ban list, just weeks before the [North American Regional Championship in Las Vegas](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/organizedplay/north-american-regional-championship-info/) on December 11-13. Riven, Kennen and Kai'Sa were all prominent at the European Championship in Stuttgart, and decks built around each will have to adjust.

For [Frites Esports Club](https://www.fec.gg/) players Guukboii and Arneej, that change is a chance for a different competitive landscape. Guukboii expects the dominant Stuttgart decks to lose ground, while Arneej sees new opportunities for Master Yi and less familiar strategies.

## What exactly was banned on November 23?

Riot's [official November ban announcement](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/announcements/radiance-ban-list-updates-effective-november-23-2026/) names eight cards and two battlefields. Several were important tools in the strongest Stuttgart strategies.

The Riven-related changes include [Riven, Broken Blade](https://riftbound.gg/cards/rad-043-riven-broken-blade/), [Zhonya's Hourglass](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-077-zhonyas-hourglass/) and [Stellacorn Herder](https://riftbound.gg/cards/sfd-048-stellacorn-herder/), alongside the battlefields [Star Spring](https://riftbound.gg/cards/unl-215-star-spring/) and [Emperor's Dais](https://riftbound.gg/cards/sfd-207-emperors-dais/). Kennen loses access to [Nocturne, Horrifying](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-194-nocturne-horrifying/) and [Rhasa the Sunderer](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-195-rhasa-the-sunderer/), while Kai'Sa is affected by the ban on [Brynhir Thundersong](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-026-brynhir-thundersong/). The remaining banned cards are [Dazzling Aurora](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-160-dazzling-aurora/) and [Baited Hook](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogn-242-baited-hook/).

## Why did Riot make such a big change?

The Stuttgart results explain much of the urgency. According to Riot, **Riven accounted for 43% of registered decks** at the European Regional Championship. Riven decks took three of the top four places and eight of the top sixteen, while posting a win rate of around 60% or higher against opponents outside the mirror match.

Kennen was also performing exceptionally well. Around 15% of Stuttgart players registered Kennen, yet the Legend claimed three top-eight spots and won the entire event. Riot highlighted the pressure created by early Nocturne plays and powerful Rhasa turns.

The changes are not only about Stuttgart. Riot also identified effects that create unhealthy play patterns or could become more problematic with future sets. That is why Baited Hook, despite seeing relatively little competitive play, was banned ahead of upcoming releases.

## Is Miracle Kennen finished?

Guukboii believes the **Miracle Kennen deck seen in Stuttgart is effectively finished**. In his view, losing two key pieces means that replacing a couple of cards will not recreate the same strategy. That does not mean every Kennen deck is gone: he thinks a competitive version, if it exists, would be a different archetype that players have yet to discover.

This perspective comes from someone who relied on those cards himself. In [Guukboii's full Stuttgart recap](/blog/riftbound-eu-regionals-guukboii-kennen-comeback.html), Nocturne and Rhasa both feature in important moments of his 8-4 run. The question now is whether Kennen can find a new game plan rather than trying to preserve the old one.

## Could Irelia replace Riven?

Guukboii is more decisive about Riven. He considers the pre-ban version far too strong and expects the changes to hit it hard enough that **Irelia may become the stronger Legend in those colours**.

That is his prediction, not an established post-ban ranking. The loss of Riven, Broken Blade and the changes to supporting tools and battlefields could make the old approach harder to reproduce. Whether Irelia actually takes over depends on what players build after November 23.

Riven's presence was hard to miss in Stuttgart. Guukboii faced Riven in five of his twelve rounds, while fellow Frites player Arneej encountered it six times across the main event and Nation Clash, as covered in [Arneej's Stuttgart recap](/blog/riftbound-eu-regionals-arneej-nation-clash-medal.html).

## Does Master Yi come out ahead?

Arneej sees the bans as a **net positive for his Proving Grounds (PG) Master Yi deck**, built around [Master Yi, Wuju Bladesman](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ogs-019-master-yi-wuju-bladesman/), which he has been playing. That might sound surprising, because losing Zhonya's Hourglass and Star Spring also hurts parts of its own game plan.

For Arneej, the benefits outweigh those losses. He expects the deck to be better positioned now that it no longer has to face threats such as Dazzling Aurora, Stellacorn Herder and Rhasa, or the [Astral Heron](https://riftbound.gg/cards/ven-044-astral-heron/) and Zhonya's Hourglass interaction that could protect a major threat. These are improvements to its expected matchups, not proof that Master Yi will become one of the best decks.

## Which decks will find their place?

Arneej has also started testing other archetypes, including Dragon decks, which look promising to him after the bans. He still sees a clear weakness against purple decks, so their success could depend heavily on which strategies become popular.

Guukboii shares the sense that the format is opening up. He describes the post-ban meta as the "Wild West", with more than ten Legends that could theoretically compete at a high level, even if each has its weaknesses. He expects a more diverse field than Stuttgart if a major event took place immediately. Arneej is similarly optimistic and expects Las Vegas to offer a substantially better competitive environment.

For now, these remain player predictions. The bans only took effect on November 23, so there are no major tournament results under this specific list yet to show which decks will actually rise.
